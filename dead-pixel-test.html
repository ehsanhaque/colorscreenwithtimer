How to Test Your Monitor for Dead Pixels

  1. Clean your screen first. Wipe off dust and smudges so they are not mistaken for a dead pixel.
  2. Open the color screen tool and press the F key (or the Fullscreen button) to fill the whole display.
  3. Cycle through solid colors. Switch between white, black, red, green and blue, one at a time.
  4. Inspect closely. Look for any dot that stays the wrong color on each background. Move slowly across the whole screen, including the corners.
  5. Check for backlight bleed. On the solid black screen, watch for lighter glowing patches near the edges.

Dead Pixel vs. Stuck Pixel: What's the Difference?

Dead pixel

A dead pixel gets no power and stays black on every color, showing up most clearly against a white screen. Dead pixels are usually a permanent hardware fault and cannot be revived.

Stuck pixel

A stuck pixel stays fixed on a single color — often red, green or blue — because one of its sub-pixels is always on. Stuck pixels are easiest to see against a solid color that contrasts with them, and they can sometimes be fixed.

How to Try to Fix a Stuck Pixel

  • Cycle colors rapidly. Flashing different solid colors over the stuck area can sometimes stimulate the sub-pixels back to life.
  • Gentle pressure. With the screen showing a solid color, place a soft cloth over the spot and apply very light pressure while the pixel cycles.
  • Be patient. These methods may take several minutes and do not always work. Never press hard, as you can damage the panel.

If a pixel stays black no matter what, it is dead rather than stuck, and the best option is usually a warranty replacement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a dead pixel test?

A dead pixel test fills your screen with solid colors one at a time so you can spot any pixel that fails to display the correct color. Dead pixels stay black, while stuck pixels stay a single color such as red, green or blue.

What is the difference between a dead pixel and a stuck pixel?

A dead pixel receives no power and stays black on every color. A stuck pixel stays fixed on one color because one or more of its sub-pixels is always on. Stuck pixels can sometimes be revived, but dead pixels usually cannot.

Can I fix a stuck pixel?

Sometimes. Rapidly cycling colors on the affected area, or gently massaging the spot while the screen shows a solid color, can occasionally unstick a stuck pixel. Dead pixels that stay black are usually a hardware fault.

Is this dead pixel test free?

Yes. The test runs entirely in your browser with no download or sign-up, and works on any monitor, laptop, phone, tablet or TV.
